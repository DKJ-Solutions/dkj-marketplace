# Fiona 💰 — canon: personal financial planning

> The portable canon of the craft (#4516), read on demand at the start of a financial task, never
> always-on. General professional knowledge only, never personal investment advice. Lookup order: this
> page, then the hub's source store where the lens names one, then the web. Tax and benefit rules change
> every year: a figure from them is always checked against the current year's source.

**Grades.** **A**: a consensus standard, a guideline, or a systematic review or meta-analysis.
**B**: a landmark primary study or a widely used professional handbook. **C**: influential, but
contested, limited to one population, or expert opinion.

| Source | Key finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| Markowitz (1952), *Portfolio selection*, Journal of Finance | Risk is judged on the portfolio, not per holding; diversification lowers risk without the same loss of expected return. | A |
| Sharpe (1991), *The arithmetic of active management*, Financial Analysts Journal | Before costs the average actively managed euro earns the market return, so after costs it must earn less. | A |
| S&P Dow Jones Indices, *SPIVA scorecards* (yearly, Europe edition included) | Over 10–15 years the large majority of active funds trails its benchmark. | A |
| Barber & Odean (2000), *Trading is hazardous to your wealth*, Journal of Finance | The households that traded most earned about 6.5 percentage points a year less than the market. | B |
| Kahneman & Tversky (1979), *Prospect theory: an analysis of decision under risk*, Econometrica | Losses weigh about twice as heavily as equal gains, which explains selling in a dip and holding losers. | A |
| Thaler & Benartzi (2004), *Save More Tomorrow*, Journal of Political Economy | Committing future raises to saving, automatically, roughly quadrupled participants' savings rates. | B |
| Nibud, *budget and buffer reference figures* (NL, updated yearly) | The Dutch reference for what a household of a given make-up spends, and how large a cash buffer it needs. | A |
| Belastingdienst, *box 3 (savings and investments)* (NL, current year) | How assets are taxed changes year by year; read the current year's rules before any calculation. | A |
| Bengen (1994), *Determining withdrawal rates using historical data*, Journal of Financial Planning | The "4% rule" for withdrawals: drawn from US history, so a starting point, not a guarantee elsewhere. | C |
