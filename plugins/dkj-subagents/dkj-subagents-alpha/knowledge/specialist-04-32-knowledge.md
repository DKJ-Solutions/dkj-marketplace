# Uma 🖱️ — canon: user research and interaction design

> The portable canon of the craft (#4516), read on demand at the start of a UX task, never
> always-on. General professional knowledge only. Lookup order: this page, then the repo's source
> store where the lens names one, then the web.

**Grades.** **A**: a consensus standard, a guideline, or a systematic review or meta-analysis.
**B**: a landmark primary study or a widely used professional handbook. **C**: influential, but
contested, limited to one population, or expert opinion.

| Source | Key finding | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 9241-210:2019, *Human-centred design for interactive systems* | Design from an explicit understanding of users, tasks and context, involve users throughout, and iterate on evaluation. | A |
| W3C, *Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2* (2023) | Perceivable, operable, understandable, robust; level AA is the usual legal and contractual bar. | A |
| Norman, *The Design of Everyday Things* (revised ed., 2013) | Signifiers, mappings, feedback and constraints decide whether a user can see what to do and whether it worked. | A |
| Nielsen (1994), *Enhancing the explanatory power of usability heuristics*, CHI | Ten heuristics that cover most usability problems found in practice; a quick expert review, not a substitute for testing. | B |
| Faulkner (2003), *Beyond the five-user assumption*, Behavior Research Methods | Five users found between 55% and 99% of problems depending on the sample; ten found at least 80% every time. | B |
| Nielsen & Landauer (1993), *A mathematical model of the finding of usability problems*, INTERCHI | The source of "five users find 85%": it holds only for a high per-user detection rate and one user group, so test small and often. | C |
| Braun & Clarke (2006), *Using thematic analysis in psychology*, Qualitative Research in Psychology | Six phases from familiarisation to report; a theme is a pattern of meaning tied to the data, not a topic heading. | A |
| Portigal, *Interviewing Users* (2nd ed., 2023) | Ask about past behaviour, not hypothetical futures; follow the user's words, and leave silence for them to fill. | C |
