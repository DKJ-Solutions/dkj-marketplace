### DEPLOY: feat/4057-why-reach-label · 20261113-134758Z

`bwj-development` gives the reach label a written reason
([#4057](https://github.com/DKJ-Solutions/dkj-marketplace/issues/4057)). An issue that carries the
reach label now says why in a `## Why <reach label>` section: who notices the defect, and what they
notice. A new PreToolUse hook, `guard-reach-reason`, refuses a `gh issue create` or `gh issue edit` that
adds the label on an admitted repo while the body the call leaves has no such section. It judges
`--body`, `--body-file`, or the issue's current body. A body it cannot read -- one built at run time,
taken from a template, the browser or an editor, or held by an unreachable tracker -- passes with a
warning. Measured in `smartwatchbanden`: 35 issues were filed with `minor` between November 1 and 13,
2026, and none said why.

**Score:** 3

#### What makes this deploy extra special

A session in a store repo that labels an issue `minor` without a written reason is now stopped and shown
the section to write, so every reach-labelled issue a colleague later reads on the board can be checked
against its stated reason.

**Score:** 4

### DEPLOY: feat/radius-three-steps · 20261113-011101Z

The brand book's radius scale is cut from six steps to three plus the pill, one per role: `--radius-1` (4px)
for a small element, `--radius-2` (8px) for a control, `--radius-3` (12px) for a container. `--radius-1-5`
folds into `--radius-2`, and `--radius-2-5` and `--radius-4` into `--radius-3`; the three old names are
retired tokens, so the house-style gate reports a use left behind. The migration table is in the brand book
README, and the Figma library's `Scale` collection carries the same four steps.

**Score:** 3

#### What makes this deploy extra special

An app that takes this brand book renames each use of the three retired radius tokens in its own layer, and
its controls, small cards and dialogs change their corners (6 to 8px, 10 to 12px, 16 to 12px).

**Score:** 4

### DEPLOY: feat/4048-mobile-back-header · 20261113-153249Z

The product layer of the brand book no longer shows the breadcrumb bar on a phone under a site
header. Below 48rem, the width the header menu folds at, the header shows a back cell
(`.site-header__back`, one level up) and the page's own name (`.site-header__title`) in place of the
brand, and the bar beneath it is hidden, also at the Phone width. A wide screen is unchanged, and a
bar with no site header above it stays at every width. `Format-SiteHeaderHtml` takes `-Back` and
`-PageTitle`, `Get-SiteHeaderBack` picks the parent page, and `Format-ProductPageHtml` passes both on
every page below home.

**Score:** 3

#### What makes this deploy extra special

A DKJ-Solutions product that takes this release gets a phone layout with a back button and the page
title instead of a breadcrumb trail that does not fit. An app that draws its own site header must add
the back cell and title to every page below home in the same update, or its phone pages lose their
way up.

**Score:** 3

### DEPLOY: feat/4054-asana-prio-score · 20261113-140315Z

`report-issue` now scores every Asana task it creates. A new optional seam, `Get-AsanaPrioFieldGid`,
names the board's `Prio-Score` number field; where it is answered, step 2 sets the score on the same
`create task` call from the card's `How urgent` line (blocking a sale 4, visible but not blocking 3,
cosmetic 2, not customer-facing 1), and `guard-asana-mirror` refuses a mirror that leaves it unset. A
ticket that came from Asana keeps its score, or gets one where it has none. Unset, the seam requires
nothing. The guard's payload read also drops a byte-order mark before the JSON, which had made its own
test runs pass without parsing.

**Score:** 3

#### What makes this deploy extra special

A store repo that answers `Get-AsanaPrioFieldGid` gets every new card scored at filing, so its issue
carries a `prio-N` label the same day instead of waiting for somebody to score it in Asana. Add the seam
to `scripts/repo-config.ps1` to take it.

**Score:** 3

### DEPLOY: feat/4066-awaiting-session-dev · 20261113-203817Z

A new parking label, `awaiting-session-dev`, for an issue that can be worked on but is too large for
one sweep turn: several independent measurements, or more than one branch. `issues-sweep` parks such an
issue with `claim-issue -Tag -Park awaiting-session-dev -Reason "<the steps left>"` instead of holding it
out with `-SkipIssue`, which wrote nothing, read to the owner as skipped, and made every later sweep judge it
again. Both pickup routes skip it, a claim warns on it, and a person or a session told to work it lifts it.
`adopt-triage-labels` now prints it among the canonical labels ([#4066](https://github.com/DKJ-Solutions/dkj-marketplace/issues/4066)).

**Score:** 3

#### What makes this deploy extra special

An owner reading a sweep's close-out sees an oversized issue parked under a label that says what it
needs, with the steps still to do in its parking comment, rather than an issue that looks skipped. Each consumer's
session-start check reports the new label as missing until `adopt-triage-labels` has created it.

**Score:** 2

### DEPLOY: docs/4051-content-product-lead · 20261113-130659Z

Brand-book rule 5 (no intro paragraph under a page's `h1`) gains one opt-in exception: a **content
product**, whose pages brief a reader as a news app does, may carry one plain `<p>` of one or two
sentences under the `h1` where the page sums up content for a reader. Its tool and admin pages keep the
strict rule. A repo opts in with the line `Content product (brand-book rule 5): yes.` in its own facts
rule; without it the strict rule holds. The core README's rule 5 and the `dkj-brandbook` skill both
carry it.

**Score:** 2

#### What makes this deploy extra special

A DKJ-Solutions product built as a reader's briefing, such as dkj-lifehub, may now open a summary page
with a short lead, once its repo declares itself a content product.

**Score:** 2
